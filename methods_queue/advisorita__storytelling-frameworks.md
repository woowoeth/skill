# Storytelling Frameworks

Six narrative structures pulled from high-performing short-form video content. Each has a fixed sequence of beats, a one-line description of what that beat accomplishes, and a fill-in-the-blank template showing how creators phrase it on camera. Use these to turn a raw personal experience, business story, or client result into a scripted narrative arc.

## How to use this skill

1. **Pick the right framework** for the story the user has (see selection guide below). If it's ambiguous, ask what the story is about and who the hero of it is (the user themselves, or a client/customer).
2. **Interview for the blanks.** Don't invent specifics — ask the user for the real details needed to fill each `(insert ...)` slot: their actual dream/goal, the real turning point, the real numbers/timeframe, the real lesson. A generic filled-in template reads as generic; the specifics are what make it land.
3. **Write the script beat-by-beat**, following the order exactly — these structures work because of the sequence (setup before payoff, problem before solution, etc.). Don't skip or reorder beats.
4. **Adapt the template language**, don't paste it verbatim. The templates show the *shape* of the sentence (e.g., "X days/months/years ago I decided to try it") — swap in the user's natural voice and phrasing rather than leaving placeholder brackets in the output.
5. **Keep the CTA/ending consistent with the user's actual goal** for the video (grow an audience, sell a product/service, get comments/shares) — several frameworks end with a call to action that should point at something real.

**Writing in Chinese?** If the user wants the script in Chinese (e.g. for 小红书/抖音/视频号), read [references/zh.md](references/zh.md) instead of translating the English templates below on the fly — it has the same six frameworks with templates already phrased the way Chinese short-video creators naturally talk, not literal translations.

## Selection guide

| If the story is about... | Use |
|---|---|
| A goal the user is still actively pursuing (not yet achieved) | **Big Goal/Dream** |
| Proving something is possible in a fixed timeframe ("can I do X in Y days") | **Challenge** |
| A specific "aha" moment or fix that solved a nagging problem | **Breakthrough** |
| Introducing the user/brand and the deeper mission behind it | **About Me** |
| A client's or customer's transformation (used for case studies/testimonials/service promotion) | **Hero's Journey** |
| A life disruption (illness, loss, accident) that led to unexpected growth | **Man in a Hole** |

If the user's story doesn't cleanly fit one framework, it's fine to blend beats (e.g., open with Man in a Hole's comfort-zone beat, close with Hero's Journey's resolution/CTA) — the point is a clean narrative arc, not rigid adherence.

## 1. Big Goal/Dream

For a goal the narrator is still in the middle of chasing — invites the audience to follow along.

1. **Introduce Dream** — Explain the origin of the dream/big goal.
   > Template: "I have (insert action), X times in my life, and ever since I was (age) I have always dreamed of (insert dream). Each time I (insert action), I think about how cool it would be to (insert dream)."
2. **Pursuit of Dream** — Explain when the individual started seriously taking action.
   > Template: "But after years of dreaming, on one random (weekday) I (insert realization/life event) and decided it was now or never, so I started (insert action/actions). I won't lie, I was (insert vulnerable feeling), but I did it anyway."
3. **Progress Update** — Share where the individual currently is on the journey.
   > Template: "So far I have already (insert action/actions), and now I am currently/about to start (insert action), which involves (insert steps). If I want to achieve (dream result) by (age/date), I need to complete this in (timeframe)."
4. **CTA** — Invite audience participation/accountability.
   > Template: "Want to see if I can get (insert action) done on time? (insert CTA)"

## 2. Challenge

For proving something is achievable within a defined timeframe or constraint.

1. **The Challenge** — State the challenge up front.
   > Template: "Is it possible to (dream result) while still (insert habit) in (timeframe)? Well, X days/months/years ago I decided to try it, and here's what happened!"
2. **The Exploration** — Break down the game plan.
   > Template: "I created a (X)-step game plan that consisted of first (step 1), then (step 2), and finally (step 3). And I followed this plan without fail."
3. **The Result** — Explain the outcome.
   > Template: "X days/months/years later, I not only (dream result), but also (side effects from result)."
4. **Realization** — Explain what the experience taught them.
   > Template: "This whole experience made me realize (realization)."
5. **Empower Audience** — Encourage the viewer.
   > Template: "Trust me — if I can do (dream result), so can you."

## 3. Breakthrough

For a specific fix or insight that solved a stubborn, ongoing problem.

1. **Problem** — State the problem and the failed attempts to solve it.
   > Template: "I was doing (solution/action) and still getting (bad result). But even after trying (actions), I kept (bad result)."
2. **Breakthrough** — State the mind-blowing realization or result that changed things.
   > Template: "...until I realized (realization)." / "...until I did this one thing that got me (result)."
3. **Fix (Takeaway)** — Break down the fix, from problem to breakthrough.
   > Template: "One day I was doing (action) when I came across (resource) that explained (solution/realization). Since I had nothing to lose, I tried (solution implemented), and X days/months/years later (dream result / social proof / positive outcome)."
   > Alt template: "Before, I struggled to get (dream result) because of (problem 1), (problem 2), and (problem 3), and had no idea how to (action). But to achieve (dream result) you actually need to (solution) — so here are the steps we followed: first (step 1), then (step 2), and lastly (step 3)."

## 4. About Me

For introducing the narrator/brand and the mission behind it — good for channel intros or founder stories.

1. **Intro** — Establish where the story begins.
   > Template: "Hi, I'm (name), and X months/years ago I was a normal guy/girl, working as a (role), and living a normal life."
2. **Conflict** — What set them on this path.
   > Template: "Well, all up until (life-changing event)."
3. **Epiphany** — The perspective shift.
   > Template: "Because of this, I realized (epiphany)."
4. **Change** — The action taken as a result.
   > Template: "Which led me to (actions), and that leads me to today. Now I (dream result), and (positive side effect)."
5. **Purpose** — The deeper "why" driving the brand.
   > Template: "(Dream result) changed my life, and I made it my mission to help (target audience) go from (pain point) to (dream result) too."

## 5. Hero's Journey

For telling a client's/customer's transformation — best for case studies, testimonials, and service promotion where the "hero" is someone the narrator helped.

1. **Intro** — Establish the hero (a client, or a type of client) and their problem.
   > Template: "X days/years ago I (or a client) was experiencing (problem)."
2. **Inflection Point** — The pain points caused by the problem.
   > Template: "Because of this, I was (pain points/symptoms of having this problem)."
3. **Rising Action** — Failed solutions they tried.
   > Template: "I tried / they tried (failed solutions attempted to fix the problem)."
4. **Climax** — Introduce the solution.
   > Template: "Finally, after X months/years of no results, I figured out the one thing that actually worked: (solution)."
5. **Falling Action** — The success that followed.
   > Template: "When I started doing this, I (results/social proof/success from the solution)."
6. **Resolution** — CTA to the business/service.
   > Template: "Because of this experience, I started (business/service) so I could help people just like me achieve (result). (CTA to business/service)"

## 6. Man in a Hole

For a life disruption (illness, accident, loss) that forced unexpected growth — classic "things weren't bad, but something was missing, then everything changed" arc.

1. **Comfort Zone** — Establish that life wasn't bad, but something felt missing.
   > Template: "X days/months/years ago I was a normal (age) guy/girl, working as a (role), and just living life. I wasn't unhappy, but it always felt like something was missing."
2. **Trigger** — The disruption that forced a reckoning (illness, accident, disruption, etc.).
   > Template: "Then, on a random (weekday), (trigger)."
3. **Crisis** — The valuable lesson learned from the disruption.
   > Template: "But this crisis made me realize (valuable lesson)."
4. **Recovery** — Putting the lesson to use.
   > Template: "So I started to (actions)."
5. **Better Place** — Life now, better than the original comfort zone.
   > Template: "Which leads me to today. Now I (dream result), and (positive side effect)."
