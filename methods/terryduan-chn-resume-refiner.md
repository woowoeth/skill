# Resume Refiner

Refine one supplied resume into one stronger version. Adapt to the candidate's field, career stage, target role, and requested format.

## Establish the scope

Read the existing resume and any supplied supporting material before asking questions. Reuse facts and preferences already provided. Identify the target role or job description, audience, language, and layout constraints. A job description is optional; without one, optimize for the stated role or the resume's existing focus.

For a review request, provide prioritized findings and concrete edits. For a rewrite request, complete the revision. For a formatting-only request, preserve factual content and emphasis except for cuts needed to meet the requested layout. Do not expand the task into multiple variants, cover letters, or a career-planning workflow.

## Preserve factual meaning

Treat the supplied resume as candidate-provided information, not independently verified evidence. Use supporting documents and explicit corrections to resolve inconsistencies. Ask only focused questions about unresolved facts that materially affect the revision; proceed with supported content elsewhere.

- Preserve official titles, dates, credentials, publication status, and individual contribution boundaries.
- Separate personal work from team outcomes, completed work from planned work, and original research from reproduction or adaptation.
- Keep metric definitions, baselines, units, evaluation conditions, and experiment versions consistent. Do not combine results from different versions or configurations.
- Do not invent numbers, tools, projects, seniority, or outcomes. Uncertain recollections do not become reliable by rounding down or adding “approximately.”
- When a result cannot be supported, describe the concrete action and scope instead. Unsupported phrases such as “significantly improved” are not a substitute for missing evidence.
- Do not interpret an unusual timeline or employment overlap as misconduct without evidence.

Maintain lightweight internal source notes for changed claims. Keep unresolved questions and editorial notes outside the finished resume.

## Select and prioritize

If a job description is supplied, map its important requirements to direct, adjacent, or missing evidence. Use its terminology only where it accurately describes the candidate's experience. Do not add missing qualifications to improve keyword coverage.

Choose content by relevance, contribution, specificity, and distinctiveness. Remove redundant bullets and low-value details before cutting substantive evidence. Place the strongest relevant sections early and use reverse chronology within experience sections.

Adapt emphasis to the audience:

| Audience | Prioritize |
|---|---|
| Engineering and technical roles | Implementation, correctness, technical decisions, reliability, and scoped performance |
| ML, data, and research roles | Problem, data, methods, evaluation, personal contribution, and accurate research-output status |
| Product, business, and operations roles | Decisions, delivery, customers, process improvements, and attributable outcomes |
| Design and creative roles | Design contribution, research, delivered work, and relevant portfolio evidence |
| Leadership roles | Actual organizational scope, people responsibilities, decisions, and team outcomes |
| Students and career changers | Relevant education, substantial projects, transferable experience, and genuine distinctions |

Use the actual role requirements for specialized fields. Do not force a universal section order, summary, font size, or page count.

## Refine skills and bullets

Retain relevant skills supported by work, projects, research, competitions, coursework, or explicit candidate confirmation. Use neutral skill names unless proficiency is established. Ordinary tools do not each need a dedicated resume bullet or public repository. Similar tools can demonstrate transferable knowledge but do not establish identical platform expertise.

Write each bullet around one main contribution:

- Action + concrete object + method + supported outcome, when an outcome is known.
- Action + concrete object + meaningful scope, when no outcome was measured.

Use precise, natural verbs. Remove vague responsibility phrases, repeated technology lists, inflated adjectives, and unnecessary jargon. Do not upgrade “contributed” to “led” or “implemented” to “invented.” Numbers and technical trade-offs are useful when supported, not mandatory in every bullet.

Prefer concise bullets that fit naturally in one or two rendered lines. Honor stricter user constraints without adding filler or dropping qualifiers necessary for accuracy. Keep terminology, tense, dates, and punctuation consistent with the underlying meaning.

## Format and verify

Preserve a working template unless a change improves readability or meets the user's request. Prefer clear headings, readable typography, selectable text, and an unambiguous reading order. Keep essential contact information out of graphics. Follow the requested submission format rather than assuming PDF or DOCX is universally preferred.

When generating files:

1. Preserve an editable source and render the requested output with available document tools.
2. Check the actual page count and inspect every rendered page for clipping, overlap, awkward wraps, and excessive density.
3. Extract text to check reading order, missing content, dates, symbols, and numerical claims.
4. Verify requested font sizes in substantive text, including local style overrides; check bullet line count in the rendered document.
5. Recheck the final output after corrections. If multiple file formats are delivered, verify their content consistency and layout separately.

Cut repetition and weak content before shrinking text. Fill unused space only with relevant supported detail; otherwise retain whitespace. If rendering is unavailable, deliver the supported text or source and state which visual checks remain undone.

Text extraction and keyword coverage are local checks, not proof of passing an employer's ATS. Do not invent ATS scores, rejection thresholds, or interview probabilities.

## Deliver

Return the revised resume in the requested format, an editable source when generating files, and a brief explanation of substantive changes and completed checks. Surface only remaining material questions. Keep audit commentary out of the application document and do not submit or publish it as a side effect of editing.
